1

The Revenue Quality Shift

Not all revenue is created equal. A dollar of recurring contract revenue is worth 2-3x more than a dollar of one-time project revenue. Buyers know this. Most owners don't.

Here's the simplest lever you can pull: convert 10% of your project revenue to recurring contracts. That's it. Not 50%. Not a total business model overhaul. Just 10%.

The Math

Take an $8M service business at a 5x EBITDA multiple.

Current state:
$8M revenue, mostly project-based
EBITDA: $1.6M (20% margin)
Multiple: 5.0x
Valuation: $8.0M

After shifting 10% to recurring:
$8M revenue, 10% on contract ($800K recurring)
EBITDA: $1.6M (margin holds)
Multiple: 6.5x (recurring revenue premium)
New valuation: $10.4M

Difference: +$2.4M in enterprise value
Same revenue. Same profit. Different structure.

Case Study: WashTec

WashTec, a German industrial services company, made this exact shift. They moved from selling car wash equipment (one-time) to selling car wash equipment plus maintenance and chemical supply contracts (recurring).

The result: their EBITDA multiple expanded from roughly 6x to over 10x between 2015 and 2020. The business didn't grow dramatically. The revenue mix changed.

Their stock price reflected it - going from around EUR 20 to over EUR 70 in that period. Same company. Same market. Different revenue structure.

Source: WashTec AG annual reports and investor presentations, 2015-2020.

How to Do It in Your Business

  • Maintenance agreements - Offer annual service contracts on every install. HVAC companies that bundle annual tune-ups see 40-60% attachment rates.
  • Inspection programs - Plumbing and electrical businesses can offer quarterly or semi-annual inspection plans. Price them to cover costs plus 30% margin.
  • Priority service tiers - Contract customers get same-day service. Non-contract customers wait 48-72 hours. The value sells itself.
  • Chemical/supply subscriptions - Pest control pioneered this. Monthly treatments on contract. It's why pest control commands 6-9x multiples.

You don't need to reinvent your business. You need to package what you already do into a predictable, recurring format. Start with your most repeat-friendly service line and build from there.

2

The Owner Dependency Test

Here's the test: Can your business run for 4 weeks without you?

Not "limp along." Not "survive with daily phone calls." Actually run - serve customers, close deals, handle problems - without you touching anything.

If the answer is no, your business has a key person risk problem. And buyers will discount your multiple by 20-40% because of it.

Per the Exit Planning Institute, owner-dependent businesses sell for an average of 2.5x EBITDA less than businesses with strong management teams. On a $1.5M EBITDA business, that's a $3.75M penalty.

The 5-Point Checklist

Score yourself honestly. Each "no" is costing you real money at exit.

  • Documented processes for every repeatable task
    Not in your head. Written down. Video walkthroughs, SOPs, checklists. If a new hire can't follow the process without calling you, it's not documented.
  • A strong #2 who can run day-to-day operations
    This person handles staffing issues, client escalations, and scheduling without your input. They make 80% of daily decisions. Buyers will interview them during diligence.
  • Client relationships distributed across the team
    If every key client has your personal cell phone, that's a problem. Transition relationships to account managers over 6-12 months before going to market.
  • Financial reporting that runs without you
    Monthly P&L, cash flow, AR/AP - generated automatically or by your bookkeeper. If you're the only one who understands the numbers, buyers see risk.
  • Sales pipeline not dependent on owner relationships
    Can your team close new business without you in the room? If not, revenue dies when you leave. That's the biggest buyer fear.

The Fix: 12-Month Transition Plan

Months 1-3: Document everything. Every process you do regularly gets written down. Use Loom videos, Google Docs, whatever works. The goal is to get it out of your head and into a system.

Months 4-6: Hire or promote your #2. Give them authority. Let them make mistakes. This is expensive in the short term and worth millions at exit.

Months 7-9: Step back from client relationships. Introduce your account manager or ops lead to every key client. Start with the smallest accounts and work up.

Months 10-12: Take 2-4 weeks off. Don't check in. If the business holds, you've done it. If it doesn't, you know exactly what still needs fixing.

This isn't about making yourself useless. It's about proving to a buyer that the machine works without you. That proof is worth millions.

The Buyer's Scorecard

Private equity firms and strategic buyers evaluate service businesses on the same six factors. Every single time. This isn't a secret - it's just that most owners never see it from the buyer's side.

Here's the exact scorecard. Grade yourself honestly on each one.

Recurring Revenue %Contract, subscription, or retainer-based revenue 30%+ High
Customer ConcentrationRevenue from top 3 clients as % of total <15% High
Average Contract LengthWeighted average remaining contract term 12+ mo Medium
Gross Revenue Retention% of last year's revenue retained (excluding upsells) 85%+ High
Margin by Service LineGross margin tracked per offering 40%+ Medium
Owner DependencyCan the business operate 4+ weeks without the owner? Yes Very High

How to Use This

Green on all six? You're in a strong position. Go to market with confidence. You'll likely command the top of your industry's multiple range.

Yellow on 1-2? Fix them before you sell. Each improvement is worth 0.5-1.0x on your multiple. On a $1.5M EBITDA business, that's $750K to $1.5M per fix.

Red on 3+? You need 12-18 months of preparation before going to market. Selling now means leaving significant value on the table. The good news: now you know exactly where to focus.

The Uncomfortable Truth

Most owners think about selling and then scramble to prepare. The smart ones prepare for 18-24 months, then go to market from a position of strength.

Every item on this scorecard is fixable. But fixing them takes time. The earlier you start, the more you keep.

A $10M business that scores well on all six factors might sell for $60-80M with a strong PE buyer. The same business with red flags everywhere might get $30M - or no offers at all.

The difference isn't the business. It's the preparation.
